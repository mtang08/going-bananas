# Race and Benchmark Policy: Block-Ending Benchmarks and Post-Event Review

**Status:** reviewable candidate guidance for package 1.2.0+privacy1; not an activated rule or medical instruction.

**Retrieve for:** `review_training_block` (Block-ending benchmark, Interpreting a benchmark, Missed or compromised benchmark) and `post_event_review` (all four sections).

## Block-ending benchmark

- Every block ends with one culminating benchmark: a time trial (an REC009 session) or an existing event. Date it in the last 7 days of the block: `planned_date` in `[review_date−6, review_date]`. Record which event or protocol will supply the result before interpreting it. (product heuristic)
- Use a standardized, repeatable protocol: the same distance as before, a similar route and conditions, and comparable preparation, for example two easy days before. Write this into the block as `protocol`, with `progress_if` and `consolidate_if` stated in advance. Those criteria make the intended comparison reviewable before the outcome is known. (coaching convention)
- Reduce surrounding load only enough for an interpretable result. It is not a full taper; see `topics/races_tapers_and_transitions.md` › Taper principles. Choose preparation for comparability rather than prescribing the example preparation to every athlete. (coaching convention)
- The weekly review places the time trial when it enters the 14-day window. The app does not enforce the date; the block records the intended benchmark and the rolling plan gives it a concrete session. (product heuristic)

## Interpreting a benchmark

- Use exactly one outcome: `progress`, `consolidate`, `recalibrate_only`, or `inconclusive`. Judge against the `progress_if` and `consolidate_if` set in advance; record the chosen outcome in the block review rather than changing the criteria to fit the result. (product heuristic)
- Compare only with the prior result from the same protocol, named in `compared_with`. Account for heat, wind, course, sleep, illness, and pacing errors. If the result is not comparable, choose `inconclusive`; an observed time difference alone cannot settle whether capability changed. (research: E-CAL-04)
- The benchmark is one strong piece of evidence alongside the training response: exposure, RPE versus intent, and symptoms. For its limits when tolerance and performance disagree, see `topics/races_tapers_and_transitions.md` › Benchmark outcomes. (coaching convention)
- Recalibrate anchors in the same block review when the result is credible and comparable (P020, P028). Do not convert one result into every zone; see `topics/intensity_calibration_and_testing.md` › Recalibration triggers. State what the performance supports and leave unsupported conversions out of the proposal. (coaching convention)

## Post-event review

- After a `target`, `benchmark`, or `supported_exposure` event, record pacing, effort and limiter, conditions, symptoms, recovery observation, a recalibration decision, and one training implication. The block's culminating benchmark is handled in the block review. (product heuristic)
- Treat the 1–3 days after the event as an observation window. Record next-day and delayed symptoms, gait, function, fatigue, and sleep before deciding. Easy movement, rest, and cross-training are all valid; choose from the observed response. See `topics/races_tapers_and_transitions.md` › Post-race transition. (coaching convention)
- A result is one piece of evidence (P027). If it is poor and confounded, defer recalibration; the result's interpretation depends on conditions and execution as well as the recorded performance. (research: E-CAL-04)
- A target event usually costs most; a supported exposure is a training stimulus, so do not over-read it. For the exposure/observation distinction, see `topics/races_tapers_and_transitions.md` › Core position. (coaching convention)

## Missed or compromised benchmark

- If the benchmark is missed or compromised, reschedule it within 14 days or extend the block. Never force it through illness or symptoms; see P004 and P018. Record the revised test opportunity or block decision, rather than silently treating a missing result as a completed benchmark. (coaching convention)
- A compromised result, such as illness, heat, or a pacing error, is `inconclusive`, not proof that the block failed; see `topics/races_tapers_and_transitions.md` › What not to infer. Preserve the context that made the comparison unusable for the next review. (coaching convention)
- For missed sessions before the benchmark as non-debt, see P013 and `COACHING_GUIDE.md` §3, item 5.
- For a return after interruption, see `topics/races_tapers_and_transitions.md` › Interruption and return to a block.
