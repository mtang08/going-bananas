# Running coaching maturity

Discovery snapshot dated 2026-11-24. Assessed from repository behavior, checked-in tests, and documented workflow. See the [data assessment](data.md#what-the-saved-reviews-demonstrate) for separate read-only evidence of actual use.

## Current coaching loop

1. The athlete asks in Codex. The skill checks the active draft and exception findings, then opens the applicable snapshot and verified research. Sunday review routes to block review for a missing or due block, a changed/unset goal race, a completed/missed benchmark, an open escalation, or an early-review trigger. The athlete can discuss alternatives in chat. [Skill](../../../.codex/skills/going-bananas-coach/SKILL.md) · [Workflow](../../../.codex/skills/going-bananas-coach/references/coaching-workflows.md)
2. Agreement authorizes submitting or revising a pending proposal. The helper invokes the local CLI for snapshots and writes, with GETs for the current draft, findings, details, and history. The app validates scope, evidence freshness, recipe and anchor references, and protected sessions. The athlete accepts all, a valid subset, or rejects through the proposal screen. A proposal can also be discarded. [Helper access](../../../.codex/skills/going-bananas-coach/references/app-access.md) · [Lifecycle](../../../backend/app/services/proposals/lifecycle.py) · [Validation](../../../backend/app/services/proposals/validation.py) · [Decision](../../../backend/app/services/proposals/decision.py)
3. Acceptance writes the calendar version and applicable block, anchor, or exception effects transactionally. Session completions and feedback provide evidence for the next snapshot. Undo creates a new plan version with protected history. Saved context includes recent proposal decisions, adjusted calendar state, block decisions and benchmark history, depending on review type. [Calendar](../../../backend/app/services/calendar.py) · [Matching](../../../backend/app/services/matching.py) · [Snapshot](../../../backend/app/services/snapshot/payload.py) · [Proposal integration tests](../../../backend/tests/integration/test_proposals.py)

The app can carry a recommendation from evidence to an athlete-approved plan and carry pieces of its outcome into a later review. Recommendation quality is a separate question from successful submission and acceptance.

Here Today refers to the retained `proceed_or_modify_today` snapshot operation. The current skill routes a request to adjust today through `exception_review`. The Today-facing section of Home is the athlete’s view, with data fetched by the app. [Skill routing](../../../.codex/skills/going-bananas-coach/SKILL.md) · [Home](../../../frontend/src/pages/Home.tsx)

| Snapshot | Nightly recovery and feedback | Findings and restrictions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Today operation | Three local dates of derived nightly recovery and completed exposures. Fourteen dates of site/side/severity/timing reports and delayed prompt metadata. | Restriction text and the 72-hour guard. Detailed findings are available separately. |
| Rolling review | Fourteen dates of derived nightly recovery and completed exposures, plus 28 dates of symptoms and delayed prompt metadata. Weekly digest and execution trends add training context. | Same restriction and guard. The exception summary adds recent finding and patch summaries, RPE mismatch counts and recurrent sites. |
| Exception review | Three dates of derived nightly recovery and completed exposures, plus 14 dates of symptoms and delayed prompt metadata. Its exception section adds recent RPE relative to intended class, with source where available. | Same restriction and guard. Open findings include bounded evidence, patch state and upcoming classes. `section_window`, `build_payload`, `_symptoms`, `_exceptions_section`, and `_exception_summary` define these projections. All three can include fatigue and illness inside nightly metrics, with the latest non-null value per field on that local date. Gait is exposed as a recent guard flag and can be explained by a finding. The snapshots do not include a complete dated check-in timeline with tags, gait, and fatigue together, and answering a delayed prompt primarily surfaces its status and any separately reported symptoms. `recovery` contains calculated per-night rows with sleep, HRV, resting HR, per-signal baseline/comparability data, recent subjective evidence, and quality metadata. The 7-day recovery entry in `load_shape` is a separate coverage rollup with counts and missing dates. The `recovery_nights` drill-down gives a longer date window of the same calculated night rows, including status and quality. The exception detector independently consumes adverse nights. Its convergence rule needs at least two adverse nights to include the recovery-marker signal, plus another qualifying signal and high fatigue or elevated RPE. Nightly rows containing adverse signals or outer quality flags alone therefore do not enable this conditional topic. ### Limits of restriction enforcement

`clinical_restriction` normalizes current and legacy profile text. `build_payload` exposes the text under `goals_and_constraints.clinical_restriction` for all three operations. A legacy flag alone yields “restricted.” The validator receives a boolean through `collect_decision_inputs` and `load_context`. `safety_active` also treats recent reported gait change, present illness, or symptom severity of at least 6.5 as guard triggers. `_safety_violation` applies specific change predicates only from today through today + 3 days. Acceptance calls `validate_subset` and checks the same rules with current context. [Profile restriction reader](../../../backend/app/domain/athlete_facts.py#L20) · [Snapshot text](../../../backend/app/services/snapshot/payload.py#L949) · [Decision context](../../../backend/app/services/decision_inputs.py#L100) · [Guard predicate](../../../backend/app/services/proposals/schema.py#L448) · [Change rule](../../../backend/app/services/proposals/validation.py#L443) · [Acceptance](../../../backend/app/services/proposals/decision.py#L324)

Using existing synthetic proposal fixture helpers and an active restriction flag, a same-day distance-only increase from 5 km to 9 km passed both document and accepted-subset validation. A same-day duration increase failed both with `V12.safety_guard`. A duration increase at today + 4 days passed both. No proposal was submitted. These checks demonstrate why a distance cap or longer restriction communicated in the text can conflict with an otherwise valid proposal. They do not establish any current athlete restriction or real calendar outcome. [Synthetic fixture and validator call](../../../backend/tests/unit/test_proposals.py#L130) · [Existing duration guard case](../../../backend/tests/unit/test_proposals.py#L608)

A product fix to investigate is a small, explicit set of dated and reviewable constraints, paired with plain text for nuances. An acceptance check would exercise restriction-compatible and restriction-conflicting cases for distance, duration, and intensity, including later days, across submission and selected-subset acceptance. If a restriction cannot be represented, test whether the app makes it visible and prompts for clarification before a potentially conflicting change. Protect the privacy of the restriction during display and evaluation.

## What would establish maturity

- Follow an entire synthetic coaching cycle through an attempted alternative, partial acceptance or no change, completion feedback, and the next review. Assert that the coach uses the accepted result and preserves the protected facts.
- Review repeated real-world use with consent and record minimal outcome measures such as time to review, whether the athlete understood the prescription, missing feedback, nuisance prompts, and reassessment follow-through. The audit contains no such outcome data.
- Test difficult combinations, especially travel plus a key session, a recent symptom plus ambiguity about dose, a low-confidence benchmark plus a requested anchor update, and concurrent lower-body strength plus deteriorating run execution. Separate what the server can enforce from what requires a sound interpretation and a good conversation.

Repository integration tests cover many individual transitions, including partial acceptance, patch lifecycle, benchmark acceptance, stale revisions, and source drift. Those are test-source findings. This audit did not run the DB integration suite or a live coaching trajectory. ## Which training priority wins?

The current race workflow separates event **rank** from event **purpose**. Rank one supplies the block's primary race target. A performance race can receive an individualized taper, a benchmark can justify a smaller reduction for an interpretable result, and a training event can replace a normal session. This distinction also applies to a secondary race. The skill and library describe these choices. The server validates a relevant target and block outline. It cannot tell whether the chosen pre-race reduction made sense for the runner's accumulated fatigue. A useful retrospective is whether the review explained which stimulus or goal was sacrificed, and what evidence should govern the next block. [Race workflow](../../../.codex/skills/going-bananas-coach/references/coaching-workflows.md) · [Tapers and transitions](../../../running_coach_kb/topics/races_tapers_and_transitions.md) · [Block validation](../../../backend/app/services/proposals/validation.py)

For an aerobic base block without a dated race, the coach can specify a progression dimension, maintained qualities, indicators and early-review triggers. Weekly volume, long-run duration, intensity distribution and recent comparable execution already support deciding whether to add duration or hold. Repeated high effort, reported delayed symptoms or a new lower-body load can change that decision. When pace and HR anchors are weak, a clear effort-based prescription can still work. The main missing evidence is the runner's response to the previous progression. No checkout evidence establishes an individual optimal increase or a universal safe weekly percentage.
