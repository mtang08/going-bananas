# Evaluating coaching decisions over time

Usefulness for one runner has three observable parts. Can the app reconstruct the decision correctly? Was the recommendation defensible with the information available that day? Did the athlete find it practical, and what happened afterward? Each has a different denominator. A single score would conceal failures and imply a causal link between the coach and outcomes it cannot control.

## A baseline from this checkout

A read-only aggregate join found three accepted plans and two subsequent-review transitions. Three eligible runs from the earlier plans appeared in the following saved review with a completion and RPE. No app check-ins or benchmark outcomes were available. Seven discarded rolling drafts could reflect workflow, changing data, experimentation or disagreement. A discard alone cannot decide between them. This is a baseline for continuity with a very small denominator. Quality of advice and effectiveness of training remain open. [Scope of the counts](data.md#what-the-saved-reviews-demonstrate)

Two historical engineering cases offer independent expectations. Replaying the previously inspected Garmin export produced no new canonical changes. A later change to refresh logic reduced a synthetic sync from 480 metric calculations to 18. Reprocessing the same inputs should preserve weekly evidence, with no second exposure or second training stimulus. Both are relevant to the coach interpreting a quiet or partial week. Their published timings and counts are specific to their fixtures. ## Measures and interpretation

| Question and unit | Operational measure and exclusions | Decision from the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did the athlete have an executable plan? Per scheduled running opportunity | Count dated accepted prescriptions with intent, effort, dose and an applicable adjustment rule. Display missing dates, unresolved calibration and provisional dates separately. A rest day can be intentional. | Fix plan coverage or presentation before tuning coaching judgment. |
| Did training follow the prescription? Per due run and week | Freeze the accepted prescription the athlete had before execution. Compare matched completed, modified, moved, skipped, self-reported and unresolved against that denominator. Show unscheduled activity separately. If midweek edits materially change the story, also reconstruct the previous accepted commitment from versions. | Distinguish a feasible planned change from repeated missed opportunities or poor matching. Show counts with fractions. |
| Was the planned dose appropriate? Per comparable week, session type and key session | Planned and measured timer minutes/distance on matching units. Report target and observed RPE where present, hard-session count and spacing. Show unknown/estimated amounts separately. Session RPE × duration describes recorded load on covered sessions. Compute a planned load contrast only when the plan actually supplied a comparable target and duration. | If completion is good but effort on like easy runs repeatedly rises, review intensity, spacing and context. If timed dose is unknown, revisit data capture first. |
| Is development on course? Per block goal | Report available weeks of running, opportunities taken, interruptions by confirmed reason, and repeated comparable long or quality exposures. Compare a credible same-protocol benchmark, or eligible steady-run trends with pace, HR, sample count and exclusions. | Repeat, progress, consolidate or change the goal. Travel, terrain, heat, sensor, illness and other training influence performance. A race PB cannot grade a prescription by itself. |
| Were adjustments proportionate? Per reviewed material decision | Count adjustment vs keep-plan judgments, trigger, delay to review, repeated corrections and reversal. Sample decisions where a prompt fired **and** ordinary weeks with no prompt. Athlete labels a sampled judgment suitable, excessive, insufficient or unclear, with one optional reason. | Tune a rule that repeatedly prompts for an explanation the coach cannot use. Review missed material events separately. Changing plans more or less often is not intrinsically good. |
| Was the interpretation accurate? Per answerable claim | Compare a fact to trusted sources available at the time, check conflicts, units, purposes, coverage and any later correction. For ambiguous physiology, judge confidence and alternatives and keep eventual outcome separate. | Repair a source/method error or revise a reasoning rule. Disagreement with the athlete warrants investigation. |
| Did the change do what we hoped? Per accepted material change due for review | At agreement, specify one intended observable response, comparator and next check. Record observed, contrary, mixed or unknown after the window, subsequent changes and competing exposures. For example, the athlete completed a shorter long run at the intended effort and supplied no delayed response. Result is known for dose and unknown for tolerance. | Decide whether to repeat, progress, reverse or ask. Avoid counting acceptance as success or treating observational follow-up as proof of causality. |
| Did symptoms or function disrupt training? Per relevant exposure and follow-up | Count new or recurrent site/side reports, function/gait changes, confirmed training interruptions and completed/eligible delayed follow-ups. Never put missing responses in a “symptom-free” numerator. Separate return restrictions and clinician input. | Reconsider progression or seek qualified input. More reports may mean better capture. |
| Is the coach worth using consistently? Per ordinary week or requested review | Measure manual reconciliations, repeated questions, app/chat handoffs, wall time to usable plan, calls, input/output usage where exposed, and an occasional athlete judgment of usefulness. Count silent/abandoned weeks and review any known reason. | Reduce the dominant source of friction or inference cost. A long conversation the athlete chose to have is different from forced rework. |

The digest, execution assessors, recovery and aerobic metrics, versioned proposals, prompt outcomes and patches already supply much of the factual layer. Begin with an as-of query and a short manual Sunday review of these counts. Decide what to persist only when an answer cannot be reconstructed. ## Scenario set for comparing changes

Start with six cases. Use the checked-in 15 original and 24 additional reasoning scenarios as a source of expected behavior. Some refer to old operations, output fields or unavailable provider scores. Map them to the current exception/rolling/block contract and available facts before grading. Supplementary cases below can be added when the affected capability changes. None of these model comparisons was run for this audit. [Original cases](../../../running_coach_kb/evaluation/coaching_cases.md) · [C16–C39](../../../running_coach_kb/evaluation/periodization_cases.md) · [IDEA-013](../../backlog.md#idea-013--evaluations-that-catch-coaching-quality-regressions)

| Case available before decision | Acceptable reasoning and later check | Failure to catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Stable easy run, one short night vs several adverse observations plus high effort/fatigue (cases 1, 2, 27–28) | Isolated change can leave plan in place. Converging evidence warrants reviewing the next demanding exposure. State support, missingness and what to check next. | A wearable cutoff cancels a week, or repeated disagreement is ignored. |
| 2. Good device readings, worsening function after a run, an explicit dated restriction (cases 3, 4, 29) | Respect the actual restriction, including on later planned days. Ask for qualified input where appropriate. Check function/response after the exposure and leave missed reports unknown. | A valid-looking increase conflicts with the restriction, or the coach issues clearance. |
| 3. Two plans on nearby days, one moved recording, partial Garmin coverage and a later treadmill correction | Preserve unknown and manual links. After confirmation, update the right workout and review evidence exactly once. Recheck the pending revision. | False skip, double volume, unsupported rep/pace claim or acceptance against obsolete evidence. |
| 4. Missed quality day, a lower-body lift and a short travel week (cases 6, 8, 30, 35–37) | Preserve the lifting commitment, rank the key run, ask only for an unknown constraint that changes placement, and review the temporary change. | Catch-up intensity compressed into adjacent days or a lasting parity change. |
| 5. Re-ranked race, successful comparable benchmark vs an unusually fast incomparable run (C22–24, C32–34) | Explicitly revisit target, block intent and anchor evidence. Withhold calibration when protocol, setting or effort are insufficient. | Goal time silently becomes current training pace, or all anchors change after one observation. |
| 6. Reopened chat after partial acceptance of a change, a rejected alternative and a due next check | Recover the actual accepted dose, its reason and known response. Reuse the stable draft when appropriate. A missing follow-up is a valid conclusion. | Recommending a rejected change as if accepted, forgetting why the last change happened, or re-asking known context. |

For the rehab iteration, compare a day when the athlete completed both run and rehab with one when they completed only the run. Include variants with an unanswered symptom question, two short sessions that together exceed the daily budget, and a review date that passes without confirmation of a restriction change.

## Retrospective method and release decision

1. **Freeze time before reasoning.** For a real historical review, read its immutable saved snapshot, approved evidence selection/versions and proposal revision locally. Label historical holes. A newly built snapshot with an old date can still see later edits and current database state. Where an older input is missing, mark it unreplayable. Keep the later actuals and the historical coach answer hidden during first judgment.
2. **Create matched synthetic cases.** Abstract the decision structure into fictitious values and dates while retaining conflicts, unknowns and timing. Use separate holdout variants for sparse inputs, normal weeks and changed restrictions. The existing backlog limits model-in-the-loop evaluation to synthetic data and on-demand execution. Private aggregate retrospective analysis can stay local without sending real payloads to model evaluators.
3. **Check the deterministic path first.** Establish expected accepted session, provisional horizon, quality flags, scope, selected research, freshness, and result after a correction/retry. For a small full journey run helper → pending → partial acceptance → synthetic recording → follow-up → new snapshot against the isolated test/demo workspace. Verify the accepted calendar and digest independently of the proposed document. [Integration fixtures](../../../backend/tests/integration/) · [Browser decision journey](../../../e2e/p0b.spec.ts)
4. **Compare current and candidate judgment.** Pin the model, effort, skill, KB digest, evidence packet, tool budget and clock. Vary one change at a time. Hide model labels and randomize response order. For borderline cases run more than once to expose variation. Grade factual grounding, preservation of constraints, proportionality, dose clarity, appropriate uncertainty and a feasible next check. Record pass/concern/fail for each with a reason. Multiple prescriptions can pass. The athlete judges practicality. Medical questions go to a qualified professional.
5. **Inspect the disagreement.** Trace it to missing data, retrieval, interpretation, policy, user preference or presentation. Inspect at least one correct no-change response alongside exceptions. A fabricated observation, unaccepted plan mutation, missed explicit restriction or unsupported clearance blocks adopting a candidate. Small style differences can be settled by the athlete.
6. **Test ordinary use.** Compare several consecutive reviews against the baseline with the measures above. Preserve the selected implementation, input versions and examples of both useful and unhelpful advice. Accept lower-cost routing only if the safety-critical cases pass, ordinary recommendations stay useful and measured review cost/latency improves. N-of-one before/after comparisons inform product choices. Training adaptation has many causes.

One wrong unit should fail a deterministic check every time. One slower 5K should lead to an investigation, and may leave the coaching recommendation entirely defensible.
