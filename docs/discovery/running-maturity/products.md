# Product patterns worth borrowing

Primary product documentation checked 2026-11-24. The question was what helps this athlete complete, interpret or change a run over repeated weeks. These are documented capabilities on the named surfaces. We did not use paid accounts, pair a watch or find comparative evidence here that one product improves performance or prevents injury more than another. The proposed benefits below are hypotheses for local evaluation.

| Product and verified workflow | What to take into Going Bananas | Limit or tradeoff |

| **Runna.** Pace Insights compares target-relative reps across the five most recent eligible speed workouts. Plan Realignment offers different choices after an interruption, including extending a flexible plan or retaining a fixed end date. [Pace chart](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/10751290-pace-insights-chart) · [Realignment](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/10026375-how-to-use-the-plan-realignment-feature) | When proposing faster reps, show the few comparable sessions, target version and exclusions. When training resumes, distinguish a flexible base block from a fixed race date. | Runna's chart omits missed/incomplete reps and excludes outliers. A fast set of surviving reps could hide an unfinished session. Its realignment prompt also depends on how the athlete marked missed workouts. Use local completion and coverage semantics. |
| **Garmin Coach and race features.** Compatible devices deliver the scheduled workout at activity start. Adaptive plans take account of schedule, fitness and race date. A primary race can guide suggestions when multiple events exist. [Watch plan workflow](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-FABAD210-C716-48B1-AB1D-A09097F1CED4/EN-US/GUID-A2FB338B-0E75-4149-A5EE-BA66064D2ABF.html) · [Primary race](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-676967A0-1B23-4384-9BC9-76F3D643F1C8/EN-US/GUID-DF6FE8CB-4039-4BC9-B040-E310A4BDA966.html) | Put purpose, effort, time and first adjustment where the athlete starts the session. Keep the existing rank-one race visible during a goal change. | Device support varies. Garmin lists the Personal API used here as pull-only. A watch feature or partner's integration does not establish a local workout-publishing path. The athlete already declined watch delivery. [Developer tiers](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/overview/) · [Recorded preference](../../backlog.md#deferred-follow-ups-from-shipped-features) |
| **TrainingPeaks.** A workout card compares actual with planned duration, distance or training stress and exposes the measure driving its color. Premium mobile views pair subjective effort and feeling over time, with a drill-down to the workout. [Workout card](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/204861204-Workout-Card-Overview) · [Athlete insights](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/41077784209165-Athlete-Home-Performance-Insights) | Make the comparison specific. An easy run may hit duration and feel unusually hard. Show both facts and which is relevant to tomorrow's decision. | A dose ratio can describe adherence without telling whether the runner achieved the intended stimulus. Collecting a second feeling scale is valuable only if the existing RPE, fatigue and symptom answers leave a real decision unresolved. |
| **Athletica.** Its revised Workout Wizard separates a shorter session from a lower-load session and recovery. The product computes explanations of duration/load changes directly. Its designers document earlier problems with insufficiently easy alternatives and choices reverting after replanning. [Workout Wizard design and limits](https://athletica.ai/blog/the-new-workout-wizard-training-that-meets-you-where-you-are) | For a disrupted key run, compare the intended stimulus and the sacrificed dose of one feasible alternative. Show its effect on nearby long runs and lower-body lifts before acceptance. | A shorter interval session can still be demanding. Swaps require distinct athlete reasons, confirmed options and preservation of the accepted choice. A large automatic workout generator would add little to an occasional exception. |
| **HumanGO.** Its guide distinguishes recurring availability, a customized week, and a time-limited illness/recovery period. Athletes can shift sessions, log how they feel, inspect post-activity feedback and enable or disable automatic replanning. [Onboarding and plan controls](https://humango.ai/newtotraining) | Give a disruption an end or review date and show the first normal week after it. Derive the next available slot from confirmed constraints. | Continuous replanning and daily input add decisions. The owner prefers an athlete-initiated Sunday review and a stable accepted plan. An ordinary busy day should be cheap to report and cheap to leave alone. |
| **Intervals.icu.** Its maintainer documents how activity and weekly compliance are calculated, the fallback from load to time, and an ignore option for an unpaired warm-up. [Maintainer's compliance specification](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/compliance-activity-field/47805) | Publish denominators, excluded exposures, and the plan version beside adherence. Distinguish a separated recording, a self-report, a planned omission and missing coverage. | The published formula can assign 100% when neither target duration nor load exists. Here the correct actionable result is an unknown dose comparison, even if completion is known. |
| **Final Surge.** Its mobile release documents workout-linked pain location and severity with a body graphic and history available to the coach. [First-party release notes](https://site.finalsurge.com/updates) | Reuse a previously reported site and side when a delayed response actually matters. Show explicitly answered, unasked and unanswered follow-ups separately. | A body map adds UI and coding burden for one athlete. Site, timing, function and ambiguity are more relevant to the next run. The product description provides no clinical validation of causal attribution. |

## Where local ownership pays off

A commercial product can already put a structured workout on a wrist. A runner returning from a shortened long run should be able to ask “What did we agree to change, which lifts and restrictions were in effect, what did I report the following day, and did we check before adding the minutes back?” Much of the needed data already exists locally. The proposed value is fewer repeated questions and a better-informed progression decision. Test those outcomes before buying data, building dashboards or connecting another coach. [Current coaching loop](coaching.md) · [Opportunity inventory](opportunities.md) · [Evaluation measures](evaluation.md)

Two primary research examples illustrate the limits of transferring product claims. A trial of 40 recreational runners tested timing demanding sessions using individualized morning HRV over a defined training period. Its design does not validate a daily cutoff for this watch's nightly HRV. An exercise trial in 36 adults with clinically staged tibialis posterior tendinopathy measured pain and function under a specific intervention. It gives a reason to preserve function and intervention provenance, but cannot set this athlete's return-to-run dose or clearance. Carry unresolved decisions through the existing KB intake before changing runtime guidance.
