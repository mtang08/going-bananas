## Structured races, broader goals and anchors

The race event has a date, optional distance, purpose (`pr`, `benchmark`, `training`), optional goal time and result, lifecycle status, and versioned upcoming rank. Rank one is the current top race. The coach snapshot carries both target seconds and pace derived from target time and distance. An accepted block can reference a target event and store a build outline. Ranking can change after acceptance, and the app exposes that mismatch. Existing tests cover reordering, conflicts, active block target status and event-linked block history. [Models](../../../backend-go/sql/schema.sql), [event logic](../../../backend-go/internal/planning/events.go), [planning routes](../../../backend-go/internal/planninghttp/http.go), [API tests](../../../backend-go/internal/planninghttp/http_test.go), [snapshot tests](../../../backend-go/internal/coaching/snapshotreads/inputs_test.go)

Broader goals carry text, horizon, priority and status. Their prose can record an intent with no structured milestone, deadline or measurement link. Event result seconds are distinct from a benchmark activity and a calibrated anchor. A benchmark result can link an event and activity, with manual matching available, and an anchor can reference an event or an activity with explicit protocol, confidence and validity dates. The general event row has no official-result provenance, course or surface, or activity pointer. Add race-specific fields only when a concrete race decision needs them. A race result and a measured threshold remain different kinds of evidence even when either could help estimate the other. [Models](../../../backend-go/sql/schema.sql), [benchmark service](../../../backend-go/internal/blockbenchmark/selection.go), [planning routes](../../../backend-go/internal/planninghttp/http.go)

Historical migration `0022_race_goals` deleted prior event records after clearing affected references. It is evidence of a previous contract change, with deleted records recoverable from the applicable backup. The current schema does not establish how much live race history is available. ## What the saved reviews demonstrate

A local PostgreSQL read-only aggregate check on 2026-11-24 found three accepted proposals that created calendar versions, two block reviews and one rolling review. Seven other rolling proposals were discarded. Across the two transitions from one accepted proposal to the next, three non-deleted, non-placeholder run sessions revised by the earlier acceptance were scheduled from that acceptance through the day before the next saved review. All three had a linked actual, and all three appeared in the next accepted proposal’s stored snapshot with completion status and RPE. One of the two next snapshots also carried a weekly digest. The check used saved proposal, calendar, completion and snapshot rows, returning counts only. [Proposal storage](../../../backend-go/sql/schema.sql), [calendar storage](../../../backend-go/sql/schema.sql), [snapshot projection](../../../backend-go/internal/coaching/snapshots)

This establishes a repeated execution-to-review data path on a small sample. No app check-ins or benchmark outcomes were present. Training response beyond recorded effort, changes in coaching judgment because of feedback, and the quality of those decisions remain unverified. Discard counts alone cannot explain why drafts were dropped.

Stored context measurements covered 25 snapshots across five operations. Rolling review had 13 snapshots, with a median of about 27,000 proxy tokens and the latest at about 35,000. Block review had seven snapshots, with a median of about 46,000 and the latest at about 55,000. The store spans different snapshot generations and includes some much larger historical estimates. Proxy counts describe context size. Actual model usage, conversation overhead and cost were outside the read. ## What counts as a successful workout?

| Session purpose | Evidence to use first | Decision or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Easy/base | Accepted intent, timer minutes, perceived effort, drift or metres/heartbeat on eligible steady portions, comparable recent examples. | Keep easy effort sustainable. A slower easy pace with the same effort in heat does not by itself warrant recalibration. |
| Long run | Duration or distance actually completed, longest recent comparable exposure, week's other work, reported delayed response and gait. | Repeat, extend one dimension, or consolidate. Longest exposure helps choose a comparator. A missing next-day report leaves tolerance unknown. |
| Intervals | Planned work and recovery steps versus completed reps, work time, pace consistency and late change, incomplete reps, lap/target confidence. | Decide whether to repeat the dose, reduce it or progress it. HR for short reps is often unavailable by design. Never grade an interval session using whole-run average pace. |
| Tempo/threshold | Sustained work segments, duration at prescribed effort, perceived exertion, available HR/pace anchors and comparable protocols. | Adjust the next sustained dose. Hitting a pace on a treadmill with corrected splits can support execution without establishing an outdoor threshold. |
| Race, benchmark or comeback session | Confirmed event purpose, credible result/protocol, effort, context, restrictions and gaps. | A race may inform an anchor or taper review. A return session may chiefly test tolerability. Use the observed response next time, with separate attribution for intervening lifts or illness. |

These are proposed interpretation criteria for existing metrics. Availability depends on plan linkage, FIT, anchors and sampling. Compare HR and speed zone distributions separately and show time excluded. A descriptive shift in metres per heartbeat without pace, HR and conditions is hard to interpret. Reference the current thresholds, eligibility and quality flags rather than assigning a workout grade. ## Data worth asking for

1. **Prioritize the response to a specific changed dose**, directly linked to the session and collection time. Preserve “none reported,” unknown, and unanswered distinctly. The current optional exception form serializes both “No” to pain and an unselected pain question as an empty symptom list. A delayed form requires an answer in the UI, but the API model still has no explicit response field. Older empty lists cannot be safely backfilled as pain-free. [Check-in form](../../../frontend/src/pages/CheckinModalV2.tsx) · [Check-in service](../../../backend-go/internal/checkins)
2. **Capture decision-changing context** such as functional tolerance, actual restrictions with their source and review condition, temporary availability, confirmation of a significant lower-body session, and benchmark comparability. Ask when the answer can change the next session or block. Use timer/FIT/RPE and plan versions already available before asking for anything the device or app knows.
3. **Explain surprising runs selectively.** A known confounder such as heat, terrain, changed sensor or unusual fueling can explain a surprising run. Reuse existing context tags or discuss once. The symptom timeline can associate a report with an explicit or preceding run. Several preceding exposures and other explanations can matter. Treat the link as temporal evidence. [Association selection](../../../backend-go/internal/metrics/trainingwindows/symptoms.go) · [Structured tags](../../../backend-go/internal/checkins/types.go)
4. **Defer general measurements.** The checked-in product lacks a dedicated dated body-weight series or verified weight ingestion for coaching. IDEA-001 requests birthday, height and weight. Consider a voluntary, unit- and date-qualified measure only for a defined decision. General demographics, calorie diaries, cadence targets, daily composite readiness and the export's many unmapped health fields would add inputs with uncertain incremental decision value. Keep detailed lifting loads in Strong and nutrition assessment outside the app unless a specific recurring running problem justifies a change.
