# Choosing a review

| Review | When | Operation | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly (Sunday) review | The Sunday entrypoint below | `review_rolling_plan`, or `review_training_block` when due | Through the block review date, at least 14 and at most 42 days; days 1–7 committed, the rest provisional |
| Exception review | The app recommends it (a caution or hold finding, or a patch reassessment), or the athlete reports a material change | `exception_review` | Committed window (days 1–7) only; never edit provisional days (day 8 onward) |
| Block review | No active block, the review date is 7 days away or less, the benchmark is completed or missed, an early-review trigger, or a "Needs bigger review" finding | `review_training_block` | The block, and every day through its proposed review date (at least 14, at most 42 days) |
| Post-event review | The app shows a race review due | `post_event_review` | Days 1–7 |

Other requests, only when asked for exactly: `select_quality_session`, `recalibrate_intensity`, `handle_goal_or_race_change`.

Use the fresh snapshot's actual scope and server schema as authority. `snapshot_meta.scope` is the change window; Sunday, block, and goal reviews also carry `snapshot_meta.horizon` with `min_end`, `max_end`, the `rule` that set the end, and `block_review_due`. Keep the first week committed and later days provisional. Explain dose, spacing, strength interaction, and uncertainty from the returned facts and research. A missing stream, RPE, recovery night, or calibration is not a normal value. Do not merge HR and speed into a single physiology score.

Two snapshot sections describe trends. `quality_progression` lists recent sessions with the same work steps (`signature`, for example `ws1:4xt300` = 4 × 5 min). Use it to repeat, progress, or consolidate a workout: compare work pace, work HR, rep variation, and last-versus-first change only across `available` or `partial` points, and say how many sessions the comparison rests on. HR is absent for reps under 3 minutes; that is unknown, not normal. `aerobic_efficiency` reports metres per heartbeat and heart-rate drift on steady easy and long runs, always next to mean pace and HR. It is a descriptive trend under comparable conditions, not a fitness score, readiness gate, or threshold (KB E-DATA-02, policy P015); heat, hills, fatigue, and sensor error change it. Do not change training because of one run's value.

If recent symptoms, altered gait, illness, or clinical restrictions are present, honor the snapshot safety guard and avoid increasing near-term running load. Describe uncertainty and escalation without inventing a diagnosis or clearance. If an accepted target depends on a proposed anchor, the app requires reviewing that anchor alongside its target changes.

## One Sunday entrypoint

The athlete should not have to choose a protocol operation. First check for an active proposal using the main workflow. For a new Sunday review, inspect `snapshot --operation review_rolling_plan`: its `block` contains the active block, including `review_date`, or is null. For routing, read only its block, date fields, and `snapshot_meta.horizon`; read metrics and KB excerpts from the snapshot for the selected operation. If no block exists, `snapshot_meta.horizon.block_review_due` is true (the review date is 7 days away or less, including overdue), or reported evidence meets an early-review trigger, get a fresh `snapshot --operation review_training_block` and prepare that operation instead. That review judges the block with the benchmark result in hand and plans the days after the review date under the next block. Also route to `review_training_block` when `exception_summary.open_escalations` is above zero, or when `block.benchmark_status.status` is `completed` or `missed`. A block without a benchmark (`not_set`) is not a reason for a block review. Never put block changes into a rolling-plan proposal. For an existing draft, inspect its document and refresh with the correct operation if the scope must change, preserving its ID and obeying live revision validation. Do not read raw app files or invent a block query.

With no active block, establish the objective, start/review dates, progression dimension, completion indicators, maintained qualities, constraints, and early-review triggers from the athlete's priorities and evidence. Propose a 4-week block by default; choose 3–8 weeks when the objective or a dated event argues for it, and set `review_date` on the Sunday that ends the block's last week, so block reviews fall on Sunday reviews. When an active block is being reviewed, the next block starts the day after its current review date. Plan every day from today through the proposed review date; the scope stops at 42 days, and later reviews fill a longer block's tail. Every session in a block-review proposal, including the current block's remaining days, must use a recipe in the new block's `eligible_recipe_ids`. A proposed block becomes active only after the athlete selects and accepts its block change in the app. A regular weekly review adapts the rest of the accepted block, through its review date and at least 14 days; it does not silently replace the objective. A due block review assesses response and indicators before continuing, consolidating, or proposing a new objective. Elapsed block time is calendar context, not proof that the objective is achieved.

Keep days 1–7 committed and every later day provisional; these rolling windows need not align with Monday–Sunday grid rows. Saved per-session provisional flags are authoritative. Detail follows distance from today:

- Days 1–14: the full workout prescription in each proposed session.
- Day 15 onward: `recipe_id`, `intent`, and `planned_duration_s`, with `structure` null. The Sunday review that brings a session into days 8–14 adds its structure.

A Sunday review changes a session after day 14 only when an observed fact changes its purpose or dose (a missed or failed key session, symptoms, recovery, or the athlete's feedback), a schedule change or event affects it, the benchmark must be placed, or it is entering days 8–14 and needs its full prescription. Otherwise leave it alone; each change states its reason.

Identify changes versus the accepted calendar. The app's calendar exposes details; do not imply that a draft grid is accepted. Use Monday–Sunday weekly mileage in the profile timezone: recorded running plus remaining accepted runs, with time-based distance estimates labeled. Pending drafts never count toward that forecast.

## Exception ladder

Choose the lowest level that addresses the finding, in this order: current session, next session, next days (at most 3), committed window (days 1–7), then block. Reach the block level only through `escalation: review_training_block`, and keep only protective changes inside the committed window when you do.

- Patch window: at most 3 days for Logged or Caution findings, and at most 7 days for Hold or Needs bigger review findings.
- Set `reassess_on` within 7 days.
- List every committed session deliberately left unchanged in `exception.unchanged`, with a reason.
- Never edit provisional days (day 8 onward); the next weekly review owns them.
- Do not add sessions, change run frequency, or move, add, or delete strength sessions.
- Missed work is not debt; do not stack sessions to make it up.
- A no-change exception review is valid when monitoring is enough.
- Do not restore a removed hard session because one metric looks normal again; use the patch's return criteria.

## Using the exception summary on Sunday

- Read `weekly_digest` first. It holds two Monday–Sunday weeks, `weeks[0]` (previous) and `weeks[1]` (current), each with `days_elapsed`. On Sunday `weeks[1]` is complete (`days_elapsed` 7). On Monday read `weeks[0]`. For a week still in progress, compare `done` with `planned_to_date`, not with the whole week.
- For each row in `key_sessions` (quality, benchmark, and long runs), read `outcome` and the execution fields: `components` (duration, distance, step completion, target coverage; each `met`, `partially_met`, `not_met`, or `not_assessable`), `reps_completed` against `reps_planned`, `assessment_status`, `reason_codes`, and `quality_flags`. Use them to judge whether the session met its structure. `ASSESSMENT_MISSING`, `AWAITING_SYNC`, and `not_assessable` mean unassessed, not failed. `changed: true` means the session was edited after it was first planned.
  Each row also has `sport`, `moving_pace_s_per_km` (seconds per km), `avg_hr_bpm`, and `rpe` (the manual rating when available, otherwise the watch rating). Unknown values are null. Read treadmill pace and distance as estimates from the watch. Scoring omits treadmill pace and uses treadmill distance only for plans without a duration.
- `not_done` lists easy and strength sessions that were `missed` (the athlete skipped them) or `not_recorded` (nothing was recorded; ask only if it matters). Sessions removed by the athlete or an accepted patch are counted in `totals.sessions.removed`; they are plan changes, not misses. `unplanned` is recorded work that matched no planned session and counts toward actual time. A `*_omitted` count means the list is truncated; `totals` stay complete. Join a `session_uid` to `calendar.sessions` for detail.
- Unknown is not zero. `planned_unknown`, `actual_unknown`, and `activity_coverage_complete: false` mean a total is incomplete: say so and never read it as 0. Mobility appears only in `by_kind`, never in the headline counts.
- Then read `exception_summary`: the findings since the last review (a failed key session appears there as a `SESSION_FAILED` finding), patches and their status, RPE mismatches by session class, recurring symptom sites, and open escalations.
- For each patched pattern, decide whether it is resolved, repeating, or needs a block review. Resolve patches whose window has passed with `patch_resolutions`.
- Accepting a weekly review marks the open findings it saw as reviewed, except Needs bigger review findings. Mention each finding you relied on.
- Repeated related exceptions can change the committed window; they never silently change the block.

## Block-ending benchmark

- Deciding whether the block needs one (block review): set a benchmark when there is no credible earlier result or the most recent one is 42 days or more before the new block's `review_date−7`. The most recent credible result is the completed benchmark this review records with an outcome other than `inconclusive`, otherwise the newest `benchmark_history` row whose outcome is not `inconclusive`. The app enforces this (`V22.benchmark_required`; its message names the latest review date that may skip one). When a race the athlete runs as a goal or benchmark effort falls in the block, make it the benchmark even if none is due, and set the review date so it falls in the window. Set one early only when it answers a decision question: comparable workouts persistently disagree with the anchors (P020), or the next objective depends on a performance anchor. Otherwise set `benchmark` to null and put in `completion_indicators` the training indicators the block review will judge, each with its snapshot source and the direction that counts as progress: `quality_progression` (same signature: reps and pace at the same or lower RPE), `aerobic_efficiency` `w28` (descriptive only, supporting evidence), key-session completion in `weekly_digest`, and RPE versus intent (`exception_summary.rpe_mismatch`). The app requires at least one indicator when the benchmark is skipped (`V22.indicators_required`).
- Setting it (block review): choose a time trial (an REC009 session) or an existing event dated in `[review_date−13, review_date−7]`, the week before the block's last week, so the result exists at the block review. A block that culminates in a race sets its review date 7–13 days after the race. Put it in `block_change.version_fields.benchmark` with a repeatable `protocol` (same distance, similar route, two easy days before), `progress_if`, and `consolidate_if`. For a time trial, include `REC009` in `eligible_recipe_ids`; the app rejects the block change otherwise. For an event, set `event_id`; the event date must equal `planned_date`.
- Placing it (block review, guidance only): for a `time_trial` benchmark, the block review that sets it changes the run on `planned_date`, or the nearest on-cycle run, into a provisional REC009. It replaces that week's quality session; keep easy days before it and no demanding session the day after. Do not add a second run that day. Weekly reviews keep it and protect the easy days around it. Only when `planned_date` was beyond that review's scope and no REC009 is scheduled (`block.benchmark_status.scheduled_date` is null) does the first weekly review whose scope contains it place it the same way. For an `event` benchmark, use the scheduled event and do not add a REC009; keep the two days before it easy.
- Using it (block review): read `block.benchmark_status`, the `event` section, and `benchmark_history`. Set `event_review` with the benchmark's `activity_id` or `event_id`, `outcome` (`progress`, `consolidate`, `recalibrate_only`, or `inconclusive`), and `compared_with` (the prior result with the same protocol). Judge the result against the `progress_if` and `consolidate_if` set in advance, together with the training response. If the result is credible, put `anchor_changes` in the same proposal. Then decide whether the next block needs one (above).
- Without one (block review): when `block.benchmark_status.status` is `not_set`, judge the block on its `completion_indicators` against `quality_progression`, `aerobic_efficiency`, `weekly_digest`, and RPE versus intent, together with symptoms and recovery. Leave `event_review` unset and keep the anchors: the app accepts `anchor_changes` in a block review only with `event_review` (`V14.anchor_change_not_allowed`). If workouts persistently disagree with the anchors, set a benchmark in the next block or run `recalibrate_intensity`. In the chat, say what would trigger a test and the latest review date by which the next block needs one.
- Not available at the routed review: a `pending` `block.benchmark_status` whose `planned_date` has passed is not a result. If a sync is plausible (planned today or yesterday), ask the athlete. Otherwise reschedule the time trial into the handoff week (the days through the current review date), and either extend the block one week (a new version with `review_date` 7 days later and the benchmark re-dated inside its `[review_date−13, review_date−7]`) or leave the block unchanged and let next Sunday's overdue block review decide with the result. If the benchmark date is still ahead, keep it, plan the handoff week without a block change, and let next Sunday's overdue block review decide.
- If it is missed or compromised: reschedule it within 14 days or extend the block. Never test through illness or symptoms. A compromised result is `inconclusive`.
- If the athlete says the app matched the wrong activity, point them to the `Choose activity` control on the Training block page. The coach never sets the match.
